Page 01 · Product vision

A nature hike chasing chaos.

A first-person Three.js/WebGPU game about following one rubber duck through a huge-looking, intricately folded mountain river race. Roughly 100 ducks are released together. The player controls only a child running alongside the river. Water, gravity, collisions, turbulence, geometry and chance determine everything else.

THE PLAYER CAN NEVER STEER, TOUCH, BLOCK, BOOST, RESCUE, SABOTAGE OR OTHERWISE AFFECT ANY DUCK OR THE RIVER RACE.

Emotional target

“COME ON #37—NO! GET OUT OF THE EDDY! … HOW DID BATMAN DUCK GET INTO FIRST?”

The game must feel like a real sporting event occurring inside an impossibly beautiful Yosemite/redwood landscape. No rubber-band AI, no scripted winner, no hidden help for the player’s duck.

Identity

  • Serious nature rendering + absurd subject: photoreal water, granite, redwoods, mist and mountain light surrounding tiny rubber ducks.
  • Spectator-racing: the only player skill is keeping up, choosing trails, reaching overlooks and witnessing the important moments.
  • Real uncertainty: the river can create miraculous comebacks, hopeless eddies, pileups, forks and waterfall reshuffles.
  • Every shot beautiful: nature is deliberately used to obscure future river bends and compose strong views.
Page 02 · Core loop

Release. Run. Lose them. Find them again.

Festival start→Gate opens→100 ducks collide→Player runs→River disappears behind forest→Rediscover race→Waterfall / fork / rapids→Finish pond

Race start

  1. Player is assigned a numbered duck.
  2. All ducks use an equal standardized race-physics profile.
  3. Visible cosmetic shape does not alter competitive physics.
  4. Starting arrangement, water state and RNG streams come from the race seed.
  5. Gate opens and the simulation runs without player intervention.

What the player actually does

Traversal

Walk, run, sprint, jump, vault, step across stones, use footbridges, climb short trail features and take natural shortcuts across horseshoe bends.

Observation

Track the owned duck, watch standings, choose overlooks, inspect the pack, use optional binocular zoom and photo/replay tools.

Forbidden mechanics

Terrain must make hydrological sense

The river is not a flat lazy-river spline. The entire valley descends. Successive switchbacks sit slightly lower than earlier ones; waterfalls provide major elevation resets. No physically impossible crossing rivers.

Reveal rhythm

OBSCURE → RUN → HEAR WATER → REVEAL DUCKS → WATCH EVENT → OBSCURE AGAIN

The player should repeatedly lose visual contact and then burst through trees onto a cinematic view. A duck that was 51st may be 14th when rediscovered.

Player trail network

  • Trail is independent of the river and may cut directly across bends.
  • Use footbridges, fallen logs, ridges, stairs beside waterfalls, stepping stones and short scrambles.
  • Traversal changes where the player witnesses the race, never the race outcome.
  • Natural occluders also support streaming, LOD and rendering efficiency.
Page 04 · River simulation

The river is the race AI.

Separate fluid truth from water beauty. The flow solver determines velocity and outcomes. Rendering derives surface shape, foam, optics, spray and sparkle from that state.

Hybrid water stack

Authoritative river flow

GPU-friendly shallow-water / heightfield flow simulation over real terrain. Store water depth and horizontal velocity. Gravity, bed slope and obstacles create the current.

Poseidon-derived rendering

Study/reuse compatible techniques for high-end WebGPU/TSL water optics, surface detail, Fresnel, sun glitter, foam and measured-looking shading. Do not pretend the FFT ocean is itself a downhill river solver.

Volumetric zones

Use a Tiamat/SPH-style approach only where water leaves the heightfield: waterfalls, plunge pools, heavy spray and selected violent rapids.

Derived effects

Foam from flow gradients/obstructions, wet rocks from actual splash/water facts, mist from falls, audio intensity from water velocity and nearby cascades.

Simulation pipeline

terrain elevation
+ river boundary/inflow
+ gravity
+ obstacle field
      ↓
authoritative depth + velocity field
      ↓
duck buoyancy / drag / torque / collisions
      ↓
race state + finish order

authoritative water state
      ↓
presentation projection
      ↓
surface detail / foam / refraction / spray / audio

Water requirements

  • Clear shallow zones show stones, gravel and gold beneath the surface.
  • Depth changes water absorption and color naturally.
  • Rapids visibly correspond to real high velocity / obstruction zones.
  • Waterfalls can detach from the heightfield and reconnect to a lower river zone.
  • No frame-rate-dependent flow or duck outcomes.
  • Provide debug overlays for depth, velocity vectors, foam source, obstacle field and duck forces.

Page 05 · Duck simulation & roster

Ninety yellow ducks. Ten weirdos.

Roster target

~90%
classic field

Several clean Sketchfab yellow-duck models, with subtle material/number variation. Prefer efficient CC-licensed assets.

~10%
novelty field

Costume, pirate, steampunk, unusual-color or other distinctive rubber ducks. Rare enough that seeing one matters.

Competitive fairness

VISUAL MODEL ≠ PHYSICS BODY
DuckEntity
├─ stableId
├─ raceNumber
├─ visualAssetId
├─ noveltyClass
└─ STANDARD_RACE_DUCK physics profile
   ├─ identical mass
   ├─ identical buoyancy volume
   ├─ identical center of mass
   ├─ identical drag
   └─ identical collision proxy

A pirate hat, cape, helmet or different mesh must never create a racing advantage. Cosmetic appendages may animate visually but do not alter authoritative forces.

Duck dynamics

  • Sample local water depth/height/normal/velocity around the standardized hull.
  • Compute buoyancy, pitch, roll, yaw, drag and current transport.
  • Support duck↔duck, duck↔bank, duck↔rock and duck↔log collision.
  • Ducks may bob, spin, roll, wedge, pile up, enter eddies, escape, split at forks and tumble through falls.
  • Use stable IDs and deterministic ordering rules for collision resolution where needed.

Sketchfab asset policy

  • Acquire downloadable assets only under licenses compatible with the intended use.
  • Store source URL, creator, license, original filename, processing notes and attribution in the asset manifest.
  • Create THIRD_PARTY.md / ATTRIBUTIONS.md automatically.
  • Do not ship ripped game characters, trademarked novelty characters or ambiguous assets without rights review.
  • IP-character ducks may be marked prototype-only; shipping roster should use cleared originals.

Page 07 · Environment & visual language

Unreal-esque nature, not an empty demo forest.

The environment must be dense, tactile and compositionally intentional. Prefer excellent existing assets over mediocre procedural replacements. Procedural systems fill gaps and create variation.

Asset hierarchy

HERO
premium/scanned redwoods, sequoias, boulders, logs, bridge props

MIDGROUND
optimized LODs + repeated variants + procedural vegetation

GROUND
ferns, moss, needles, stones, roots, grasses, wet mud, mushrooms

DISTANCE
simplified forest masses + mountain geometry + panorama/atmosphere

Redwood strategy

  • Use the best legal photogrammetry/game-ready redwood/sequoia assets available for hero trees.
  • Huge trunks are also level-design walls and occlusion devices.
  • Generate LODs/impostors where appropriate; never draw every leaf at hero quality.
  • Use Owen's Dryad ideas/code only when license/compatibility permits, primarily for procedural secondary vegetation and variation rather than replacing hero scans.
  • Use Gaia-style deterministic grass/ground vegetation where useful.

Materials

Physically based granite, wet/dry rock response, moss, bark, soil and river gravel. Use relief/parallax approaches where helpful, but close hero geometry should remain genuinely dimensional. Texture quality must hold up in first person.

Gold

Gold flakes appear naturally in selected clear-water gravel bars and catch the sun as tiny metallic flashes. They are environmental detail, not required collectibles.

Mountain / sky depth stack

real local terrain→mid-distance granite cliffs→distant mountain mass/panorama→atmosphere / clouds / sky

Lighting & atmosphere

  • Strong sun shafts only when physically/plausibly supported by canopy and mist.
  • Waterfall spray and river mist create depth and selectively hide nearby geometry.
  • Wet surfaces darken/reflect near actual water/splash zones.
  • Restrained postprocessing: exposure, tone mapping, subtle bloom where justified; no smeared “cinematic” filter that destroys clarity.

Page 08 · Audio, HUD & spectacle

Hear the next river reveal before you see it.

Audio direction

  • No constant score during the core race. Nature and race sound carry the experience.
  • Layer river sound by local simulated velocity, depth and obstruction.
  • Waterfall low-frequency roar grows naturally as the player approaches.
  • Spatial ambience: birds, insects, wind in canopy, footfalls, roots, bridge creaks, gravel and splashes.
  • Ducks knocking together may make small plastic bumps/squeaks, used sparingly.
  • Festival and finish may use restrained music/crowd energy.

Race presentation

  • Owned duck has a clear number/identifier and optional subtle tracking marker.
  • Leaderboard can be compact and optionally hidden.
  • Picture-in-picture duck cam is optional and should not replace the pleasure of physically finding the race.
  • Finish system records exact crossing order and timestamps from authoritative simulation.

Replay

Race seed + deterministic rules + recorded player command stream enables replay. Replay cameras may include riverbank, overhead, duck-follow and waterfall viewpoints, but replay never changes the recorded outcome.

Page 09 · Harness contract

Simulation truth. Presentation contract. Three.js witness.

Fit this game into the existing 3JSE/Atlas harness rather than building a special-case architecture.

Required boundaries

src/
  sim/             authoritative water, ducks, race, player commands, seeds
  presentation/    immutable/derived snapshots + commands + debug facts
  render3d/        Three.js/WebGPU world, materials, instances, animations
  ui/              HUD, menus, standings, settings, debug panels
  assets/          manifests, licenses, normalized models/animations
  tests/           deterministic sim + integration + visual scenarios
  docs/            design, architecture, testing, performance, third-party

Binding rules

  1. Simulation is authoritative. Rendering never decides positions, rank or outcomes.
  2. No Object3D game state. Every duck/player/race object has a stable simulation ID.
  3. Fixed simulation cadence. Rendering may interpolate but cannot change truth.
  4. Named deterministic RNG streams. Separate course, roster, release, water variation and cosmetic randomness.
  5. Explicit commands. Player input becomes simulation commands; command logs support replay.
  6. Presentation boundary. Three.js reads presentation contracts, not mutable sim internals.
  7. Headless first-class. Core race simulation tests run without DOM/GPU.
  8. Witness overlays. Velocity, depth, buoyancy forces, collision proxies, rank, cell streaming and frame budgets are inspectable.
  9. Atlas semantics. Expose systems/mechanics/feel rather than file-level spaghetti.
  10. Every meaningful change is tested and browser-verified.

Page 10 · Performance & streaming

Spend detail where the camera can prove it exists.

Target

60 FPS desktop target on the intended WebGPU class, with quality scaling instead of simulation degradation.

Core tactics

  • Use forest/terrain occlusion to reduce active render interest.
  • Instancing/batching for classic ducks, stones, repeated vegetation and ground detail.
  • LOD/impostors for trees; hero assets only near important camera corridors.
  • Stream river cells and adjacent trail/environment cells around player + followed duck.
  • Keep followed/important race entities present even when outside normal visual interest.
  • Do expensive geometry processing/decoding in workers where practical.
  • Use compressed textures/meshes and explicit memory budgets.
  • Do not run full volumetric SPH across the entire river.

Telemetry

Track simulation ms, presentation ms, animation ms, render CPU, GPU frame time, draw calls, triangles, texture memory, visible trees/ducks, active river cells and SPH particle count.

Deterministic visual regression mode

Fixed race seed, tick, camera transform, exposure, weather/time, resolution and procedural visual seeds. Disable temporal randomness where possible for screenshot comparison.

Stress scenes

  • 100 ducks clustered at release gate.
  • 100 ducks colliding in narrow granite chute.
  • Grand waterfall with maximum spray + most ducks visible.
  • Dense redwood cathedral with first-person camera at ground level.
  • Long replay/soak run for leaks and sim/render divergence.

Prove the race before decorating it.

  • One descending river course.
  • 100 standardized simulated ducks with stable IDs.
  • Release gate, collisions, ranking and finish.
  • First-person player movement that cannot influence ducks.

  • Authoritative shallow-water/flow field.
  • Buoyancy and torque derived from water.

  • Poseidon-quality optical study/adaptation where compatible.
  • Foam from actual flow facts.
  • One waterfall transition with volumetric/spray zone.

4 — Folded final level

  • Build full descending switchback river.
  • Occlusion-first tree/terrain composition.
  • Player trails and shortcuts.
  • At least one strong reveal per river cell.

5 — Asset pass

  • Sketchfab duck roster + manifests/licenses.
  • Child model + normalized rig.
  • Mixamo animation library + animation director.
  • Redwood/rock/fern/ground environment assets and LODs.

6 — Atmosphere / audio / UX

  • Mountain background, mist, wetness, sun, gold, spray.

Run automated tests, production build, browser verification, deterministic screenshots, performance stress cases and a harsh visual/gameplay critic pass. Fix prototype-grade systems instead of merely documenting them.
